Like I mentioned in my [previous post](https://firetaker.com/posts/progress-update/), I paused on the core rulebook to focus on writing some *Firetaker* scenarios. This way, players can have a jumping-off point when I ask them to test the game.

I thought I'd write three, distinct scenarios highlighting three different types of gameplay:

1. A dungeon delve
2. A hexcrawl
3. A ticking-clock city adventure

Back during TripTech³, I actually started working on the dungeon delve. Since TripTech is specifically a *Mothership* trifold game jam, dozens of pages for a Panic Engine riff didn't qualify as a submission. The organizer was very kind to allow me to use a *Firetaker* trifold as my entry.

The problem? I had a now-or-never mentality for drafting the game itself, so I scrambled in the final hours of the jam to cobble together a scenario. It was tremendously bad, and I got last place in the competition. If I could go back in time and do it again, would I change anything? Probably not. I've sustained the momentum for *Firetaker* specifically because I had the rare opportunity to throw myself at it wholeheartedly alongside remarkably talented designers working on their own submissions.

*Firetaker* was playable after TripTech finished, but my dungeon delve was decidedly not. I wasn't ready to touch it again, so I figured I'd work on the city adventure or the hexcrawl. Since I had just listened to [Mystery Quest's Mythic Bastionland miniseries](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLhFDcFS50InsLzBRQtd4mRyzT4L-uLkJ-&si=OVAXtCRuKJTP4lGo), I was hyped about hexcrawls.

What would the crawl be about? The Calydonian Boar Hunt immediately came to mind. In my opinion, it is *the* original hexcrawl. The adventurers even come with their own convoluted backstories and dramatic inter-party dynamics. I wanted to do something other than a boar. First, it was going to be a giant centipede (because they're scary). Then, it was going to be a wolverine (because they're dangerous). I even briefly considered using a honey badger (because they're scary and dangerous). In the end, I remembered some Stymphalian Birds escaped when Herakles chased them off during his Sixth Labor. How neat would it be if one grew enormous and terrorized an island?

For my island, I wanted to pull from Greek Mythology with an offhandedly-named island. I'm currently reading *The Aeneid*, and Aeneas mentions the island of Olearos. As a supposed part of the Cyclades, it would be under the control of Crete. King Minos has enough baggage associated with him, so I wanted to use a different quest giver. The grandson of Minos, Idomeneus, is driven from the throne by Leucas. Leucas has a sordid origin story, but I don't know how his tale shakes out. He was my guy.

I have some tables to finish and illustrations to do, but the bones of the scenario are here. Fingers crossed I get to run it for some pals at Galaxy Con in two weeks.
